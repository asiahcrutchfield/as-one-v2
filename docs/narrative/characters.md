# Player Characters

## Nia

- Age: 9
- Residence: New Orleans
- Language: English
- Ethnicity: Black
- Nationality: American
- Playable: Yes

### Backstory
Nia is a 9-year-old girl from New Orleans. She has a best friend named Annika, who feels almost like a sister to her. They are both Jehovah's Witnesses.

Nia and Annika are inseparable. They do almost everything together: field service, playing, watching videos, and sharing their favorite songs. Their biggest shared interest is the famous Taiwanese pop group HEARTLINE, who are scheduled to perform at Jazz Fest.

(Annika is more outgoing than Nia and often encourages her to be brave. After Annika’s death, Nia sometimes imagines what Annika would say when she is scared.)

The two girls make plans to see HEARTLINE together.

However, Annika is killed by stray gunfire during gang-related violence in the neighborhood, leaving Nia devastated. She was not involved and was not the target. Nia copes by imagining that she will see Annika again in paradise. She owns a stuffed tiger named Sunny, who becomes both her pet and protector in these imagined paradise scenes.

In honor of Annika, Nia becomes determined to see HEARTLINE at Jazz Fest for both of them.

### Gameplay Role
Nia’s gameplay is a puzzle-RPG set inside her imagined paradise. She does not physically fight. Instead, she commands Sunny, her stuffed tiger imagined as a real tiger, to protect her, reveal hidden details, scare away fear, and help her move through emotional obstacles.

Nia’s sections represent real-world challenges: noticing danger, resisting panic, remembering Annika, finding courage, hiding, escaping, or speaking up when adults overlook her.

### Music Mechanic

Nia can use memories of HEARTLINE songs to steady herself when she is afraid.

The songs remind her of Annika, but they do not erase her grief. Instead, they help Nia stay present long enough to notice danger, hide, remember details, or speak.

In gameplay, this can appear as a short rhythm, breathing, or pattern-matching mechanic.

Example uses:

- Hum: lowers panic while Nia is hiding.
- Remember: triggers a memory of Annika that helps Nia process a clue.
- Listen: lets Nia notice a HEARTLINE-related detail adults miss.
- Sunny Guard: allows Sunny to protect Nia from fear, panic, or imagined danger.
- Sunny Sense: reveals hidden details inside Nia’s paradise space.

## Sunny

- Type: Stuffed tiger / imagined protector
- Connected Character: Nia
- Playable: Yes

### Backstory
Sunny is Nia’s stuffed tiger. Before Annika’s death, Sunny is part of Nia and Annika’s games about paradise.

After Annika dies, Sunny becomes more important to Nia. In her imagined paradise, Sunny is real: a powerful tiger who protects her when the real world feels too frightening.

Sunny does not replace Nia’s courage. He gives shape to it. When Nia commands Sunny, she is finding a way to act even when she feels powerless.

### Gameplay Role
Sunny is central to Nia’s puzzle-RPG gameplay.

Possible commands include:

- Guard: protects Nia from fear, panic, or imagined danger.
- Sense: reveals hidden details in the paradise space.
- Roar: pushes back fear, silence, or pressure.
- Pounce: breaks emotional obstacles or interrupts imagined threats.
- Stay: holds a space, memory, or danger in place while Nia moves.

## Antoine

* Age: 30
* Residence: New Orleans
* Language: English
* Ethnicity: Black
* Nationality: American
* Playable: Yes

### Backstory

Antoine is a New Orleans local, born and raised. He grew up in a rough neighborhood and spent much of his youth around people who survived through intimidation, violence, and illegal work. Some of the older men around him were genuinely dangerous, but when Antoine was younger, he saw them differently. They offered protection, money, belonging, and a version of manhood that made violence look like strength.

As Antoine got older, he began realizing how much that world had taken from the people around him and how easily he could have spent the rest of his life trapped inside it. He eventually distanced himself from those connections and began trying to build something different.

One of the biggest parts of that change is boxing.

Antoine begins training at a neighborhood boxing gym, initially because he wants somewhere legitimate to put his physical strength and aggression. Over time, however, the gym becomes more than a place to fight. It gives him structure, discipline, routine, and a community built around helping people improve rather than proving who is the most dangerous.

Through the gym, Antoine becomes close to Devin, a 15-year-old boxer preparing for his first youth amateur bout.

Devin is talented, competitive, and determined, but he also has a temper and cares deeply about being respected. Antoine recognizes a lot of his younger self in him. He starts helping Devin with training, holding pads for him, correcting his footwork, and talking him through moments when frustration makes him fight recklessly.

Antoine never formally sets out to become Devin’s mentor. The relationship develops naturally. Devin begins looking up to him, and Antoine finds that he enjoys helping someone else become stronger.

For Antoine, Devin represents something he did not have enough of at that age: a strong adult man who can understand anger and toughness without encouraging him to destroy himself with them.

Devin’s upcoming bout becomes important to both of them. Antoine does not care whether Devin wins. What matters is that Devin stays focused, finishes his training, and steps into the ring knowing that discipline can earn him the same sense of pride and respect that the streets promise much faster.

But shortly before the bout, Devin gets into a serious argument or confrontation with another young man. The incident humiliates him and sends his anger spiraling. Devin becomes fixated on getting revenge.

Some of the older men he spends time around encourage it.

They tell him that if someone disrespects him and he does nothing, people will think he is weak. They mock the idea that training in a gym means anything if Devin is unwilling to handle a real problem outside it.

The choice begins pulling Devin in two directions.

At the gym, Antoine tells him to control himself, keep training, and make it to his bout.

Outside the gym, the older men offer him immediate respect, revenge, and a sense of belonging.

The situation becomes more complicated because some of those men are connected to Antoine’s former life.

When HEARTLINE comes to New Orleans for Jazz Fest, members of Antoine’s old circle are hired as local fixers for a job involving festival access and HEARTLINE’s equipment. They do not tell Devin the full truth. Instead, they offer him a small favor: carry something, watch a service entrance, deliver a temporary badge, move an item, or help them gain access to part of the festival.

In exchange, they imply that they can help Devin deal with the person he wants revenge against.

Devin sees the job as a chance to prove himself.

Antoine immediately recognizes what is happening. The same kind of men who once made him feel important are now using Devin’s anger and pride to pull him into their world one small decision at a time.

Antoine tries to stop him, but he has created a problem of his own. He has never told Devin the full truth about his past.

Devin knows that Antoine used to run with dangerous people, but Antoine has kept the details vague because he wants Devin to see him as someone worth listening to. When Devin learns more about what Antoine used to be involved in, he feels judged and betrayed.

To Devin, Antoine’s warnings begin sounding hypocritical.

Antoine got to live the life he is now telling Devin to stay away from.

Their relationship reaches a breaking point when Devin decides that Antoine has no right to control him. If Antoine tries to physically stop him from carrying out his revenge or helping the fixer crew, Devin may even turn on him.

Antoine is more experienced, stronger, and a better fighter. Physically defeating Devin would be easy.

That is exactly why it would mean nothing.

Antoine’s challenge is to stop Devin without humiliating him, hurting him unnecessarily, or proving that violence is the only way a stronger man can control a weaker one.

To reach Devin, Antoine eventually has to stop presenting himself as the man who figured everything out. He has to admit that his past was not exciting, admirable, or something he escaped without consequences. He is not warning Devin because he believes he is better than him.

He is warning him because he recognizes exactly how quickly one angry decision can become the beginning of the rest of a person’s life.

At the same time, Antoine is still trying to be a dependable boyfriend to Janelle and a good uncle to Nia. His instinct is to protect all of them by keeping dangerous information to himself and handling problems alone.

That instinct repeatedly makes things worse.

Janelle sees that Antoine can teach Devin to listen to his corner while refusing to listen to anyone in his own life. Nia sees Antoine as someone strong enough to protect her, but his secrecy can leave her confused about the danger surrounding Jazz Fest.

Antoine’s story is ultimately about what kind of strong man he wants to become.

The men from his past taught him that strength meant making people fear you.

Boxing teaches him that strength requires control.

Devin forces him to learn something harder: strength can also mean staying present, telling the truth, allowing someone else to make their own choices, and helping them become strong enough to choose differently.

When the HEARTLINE crisis brings Antoine’s old world, Devin’s anger, and Nia’s determination to attend Jazz Fest into the same place, Antoine can no longer protect everyone by himself.

His real test is not whether he can win a fight.

It is whether he can become the kind of man who knows when to fight, when to hold back, when to tell the truth, and when someone else needs him in their corner.

### Gameplay Role

Antoine’s gameplay is a direct beat-'em-up style built around boxing fundamentals.

His core actions may include:

* Jab: a fast strike used to interrupt enemies and control distance.
* Combination: chained punches that reward timing rather than button-mashing.
* Guard: reduces damage but consumes stamina under sustained pressure.
* Slip: avoids a strike and creates an opening for a counter.
* Body Shot: weakens an enemy’s stamina or guard.
* Counter: rewards patience and reading an opponent.
* Footwork: helps Antoine reposition, protect civilians, or avoid being surrounded.
* Clinch or Shove: creates space when ring-style boxing breaks down in crowded environments.

Antoine is trained for controlled one-on-one sparring, but the main story forces him into messy situations involving multiple opponents, cramped service routes, improvised weapons, and civilians who need protection.

His combat should reward restraint and awareness. Reckless aggression may leave him exhausted, endanger someone nearby, or cause him to lose sight of what he is actually trying to protect.

A confrontation with Devin can invert Antoine’s normal combat. The goal is not to defeat Devin, but to control the encounter without seriously hurting him. Antoine must rely on defense, footwork, restraint, and positioning rather than overwhelming offense.

At Jazz Fest, Antoine’s flaw reaches a breaking point. He is trying to protect Nia, pull Devin away from the fixer crew, and confront people from his past at the same time.

He is eventually forced to accept that he cannot carry every problem alone.

Antoine’s biggest gameplay tension is control: he is strong enough to hurt people, but his growth depends on understanding that strength is not measured by how much punishment he can deliver.

## 陳柏安

* Age: 32
* Residence: Taipei, Taiwan
* Language: Mandarin Chinese, English
* Ethnicity: East Asian
* Nationality: Taiwanese
* Playable: Yes

### Backstory

陳柏安 is a Taiwanese police investigator living in Taipei. He married relatively young and has an 11-year-old daughter from that marriage.

柏安 loves his daughter deeply, but his relationship with her has become strained since the divorce.

He is dependable in ways that are easy to measure. He remembers school schedules, appointments, pickup times, forms that need to be signed, and things she mentioned weeks earlier. If something goes wrong, 柏安 is usually the person who knows what to do.

But he is much worse at simply being present.

Police work has repeatedly interrupted the time he is supposed to spend with his daughter. 柏安 rarely intends to break a promise. Each time, there is a reasonable explanation: a case runs late, someone needs him, an urgent call comes in, or he convinces himself that he only needs another hour.

There is always another hour.

His daughter has gradually stopped getting as upset when plans fall through. That worries 柏安 more than anger would. She is getting older, developing her own interests and friendships, and slowly learning not to expect him to show up.

柏安’s marriage ended partly because of the same pattern. He was never intentionally cruel or indifferent, but he often treated relationships like problems that could be maintained through responsibility and logistics. He could solve practical problems much more easily than he could recognize emotional distance while it was happening.

He and his ex-wife are no longer together, but they are not enemies. She trusts 柏安 to care about their daughter. What she no longer trusts is his ability to recognize when work has become an excuse for postponing the rest of his life.

At the beginning of the story, 柏安 is trying to change that.

His daughter has been asking him to take her somewhere for a full day or weekend together. The exact destination is not especially important. What matters is that she chose it, and 柏安 finally promises her that this time he will not cancel.

No work.

No phone calls unless there is a genuine emergency.

No “just one thing first.”

For 柏安, it seems like an easy promise to keep.

Then HEARTLINE enters his work.

柏安 is assigned to investigate what initially appears to be a minor security irregularity involving HEARTLINE, an internationally famous Taiwanese pop group preparing to travel to New Orleans for Jazz Fest.

The issue begins with small inconsistencies: unusual access requests, duplicated credentials, equipment records that do not match, or changes to schedules that appear harmless individually.

柏安 is especially good at this kind of work.

He is calm, observant, and skilled at seeing patterns across information that other people treat as unrelated. His instinct is to understand the entire situation before committing resources or putting people in danger.

What begins as something he believes he can resolve quickly becomes increasingly serious.

柏安 discovers signs that someone with legitimate access to HEARTLINE’s operation may be manipulating the group’s travel and equipment systems. The suspicious activity appears to involve both Taiwan and people waiting for the group in New Orleans.

The equipment is already moving toward the United States.

柏安 now faces a familiar decision.

Professionally, he has the strongest understanding of the Taiwan-side evidence. He recognizes the pattern, knows which records may have been compromised, and can explain the situation to HEARTLINE’s security personnel and the American authorities responsible for protecting the festival.

Someone needs to follow the case to New Orleans.

柏安 is the logical choice.

Going also means breaking the promise he just made to his daughter.

Again.

When he tells her that he has to leave for America, she does not yell or accuse him of not loving her. She has heard versions of this conversation before.

She simply stops expecting the trip to happen.

That hurts 柏安 more than if she had become angry.

He promises they will do it when he returns.

She tells him he does not have to promise.

柏安 travels to New Orleans as part of the investigation and security coordination surrounding HEARTLINE. He is not there to behave as though Taiwanese police authority extends into Louisiana. His role is to interpret the Taiwan-side evidence, advise HEARTLINE’s security team, coordinate information with local authorities and festival security, and help determine where the threat is likely to emerge.

The case quickly puts 柏安 in situations where his greatest professional strength becomes difficult to rely on.

Official records have been manipulated.

Antoine understands people and relationships that do not appear on any document.

Nia notices details about HEARTLINE that adults overlook and sometimes explains what she remembers through fear, emotion, and her imagined relationship with Sunny.

柏安 initially becomes frustrated when information does not arrive in a form he can organize cleanly.

He is not emotionless, and he does not believe feelings are meaningless. His problem is that he feels most comfortable when a situation can be turned into something actionable: a timeline, a route, a suspect, a decision.

That habit also defines his relationship with his daughter.

柏安 knows how to arrange her schedule.

He has been slower to understand what she actually needs from him.

As the crisis develops, 柏安 is forced to work with Antoine, whose knowledge comes largely from lived experience and personal relationships rather than official systems. Their approaches sometimes clash, but the difference between them is not simply logic versus instinct.

Antoine understands the people standing directly in front of him.

柏安 understands how the pieces around them connect.

Neither perspective is sufficient by itself.

Nia presents an even more unfamiliar challenge. 柏安 initially approaches her like a witness whose information needs to be clarified. Over time, he recognizes that listening properly means understanding how she experienced what happened rather than stripping away everything that sounds emotional or childish.

The case forces 柏安 to confront the same mistake he has been making in his personal life.

He has spent years believing that being useful is the same thing as being present.

His job rewards him for fixing problems.

His daughter does not need every problem fixed.

Sometimes she simply needs her father to show up when he said he would.

柏安’s story is not about learning that planning, responsibility, or logic are bad. Those qualities make him extremely good at what he does.

His growth comes from accepting that no plan can remove every uncertainty, and no amount of competence can substitute for being emotionally available to the people who depend on him.

When the HEARTLINE crisis reaches Jazz Fest, 柏安 must make decisions without having complete information. He cannot protect every route, verify every witness, control every person, or guarantee that nobody will get hurt.

He has to listen to Antoine.

He has to take Nia seriously.

He has to trust other people to carry parts of the situation he cannot control.

And when the crisis is over, his most important unresolved problem will still be waiting for him in Taipei.

His daughter.

柏安 cannot repair that relationship by solving one case or buying another ticket.

He has to go home and start showing up.

### Gameplay Role

柏安’s gameplay is tactical and strategic rather than focused on direct physical combat.

He gathers information, studies environments, identifies threats, controls routes, allocates limited resources, protects civilians, and creates opportunities for other characters to act.

His gameplay may involve:

* analyzing security maps and camera feeds;
* comparing access records and equipment manifests;
* identifying suspicious people or inconsistencies;
* marking possible threats;
* deciding which routes should be secured;
* directing security personnel or civilians;
* choosing where limited attention and resources should be committed;
* updating plans as new information contradicts previous assumptions.

柏安 rarely receives perfect information.

Official records may be false.

Witnesses may be frightened or uncertain.

Antoine may know something important without being able to prove it.

Nia may describe something accurately without understanding why it matters.

柏安’s challenge is not simply discovering the correct answer.

It is deciding when he has enough information to act.

His gameplay reflects his personal conflict: he is most comfortable when everything can be planned, but the situations that matter most require him to make responsible choices before certainty arrives.

## Devin

* Language: English
* Ethnicity: Black
* Nationality: American
* Playable: No

### Backstory

Devin is a 15-year-old amateur boxer training at the same neighborhood gym as Antoine.

He is talented, competitive, and physically confident, but he has a quick temper and cares deeply about being respected. He is preparing for his first youth amateur bout and takes the opportunity seriously, even if he does not always have the discipline to match his ambition.

Devin did not initially seek Antoine out as a mentor.

Antoine simply began helping him during training: holding pads, correcting his stance, showing him how to conserve energy, and calming him down when frustration made him fight recklessly.

Over time, Devin began trusting him.

Antoine recognizes a lot of his younger self in Devin. Devin is surrounded by some of the same kinds of influences Antoine knew growing up: older men who appear confident, respected, dangerous, and able to solve problems immediately.

To Devin, those men are not obviously villains.

They offer belonging, protection, approval, and a version of adulthood that seems much faster and more powerful than slowly earning respect through school or boxing.

Shortly before his first bout, Devin gets into a serious confrontation with another young man. The incident leaves him humiliated and furious.

Devin becomes fixated on retaliation.

Some of the older men around him encourage his anger. They tell him that walking away will make him look weak and mock the idea that boxing means anything if he cannot handle disrespect outside the ring.

Antoine tries to keep Devin focused on his upcoming fight.

Their relationship becomes strained when Devin begins learning more about Antoine’s own past.

From Devin’s perspective, Antoine participated in the same world he is now warning Devin to avoid. Antoine’s refusal to talk openly about what he did makes his advice sound increasingly hypocritical.

They do not tell him the full nature of the job.

Instead, they ask him to perform apparently simple favors: carry something, deliver a temporary badge, watch a service entrance, move an item, or report someone’s movements.

In return, they imply that they can help Devin deal with the person he wants revenge against.

Devin sees the arrangement as a way to prove that he is no longer a kid.

He does not understand how quickly the favor is pulling him into something much more dangerous.

### Story Role

Devin is the emotional center of Antoine’s personal story.

He represents the possibility that Antoine can use his experience to help someone become stronger rather than simply regretting the person he used to be.

Devin also challenges Antoine’s hypocrisy. Antoine wants Devin to trust him while withholding the parts of his own past he is ashamed of.

Their relationship may eventually become physically confrontational when Antoine tries to stop Devin from carrying out his revenge or helping the fixer crew.

The important question is not whether Antoine can defeat Devin.

It is whether Antoine can reach him without humiliating him, controlling him, or proving that violence is the only language stronger men understand.

## Janelle

* Age: 28–32 [tentative]
* Residence: New Orleans
* Language: English
* Ethnicity: Black [tentative]
* Nationality: American
* Playable: No
* Relationship: Antoine’s girlfriend

### Backstory

Janelle has known Antoine long enough to remember parts of the life he is trying to leave behind.

She does not romanticize his past, but she also does not reduce him to it.

She supports his boxing because she has seen how the gym gives him structure, routine, pride, and a healthier community. More importantly, she has seen another side of Antoine emerge through his relationship with Devin.

Antoine is patient with Devin in ways he is not always patient with himself.

Janelle notices that he has a genuine ability to encourage younger people and sees the possibility of a future where Antoine is valued because people feel safe around him rather than because they fear him.

Their relationship is not perfect.

Janelle’s biggest frustration is that Antoine still believes protecting people means deciding what they are allowed to know.

When something becomes dangerous, he shuts down, gives vague answers, and tries to fix the situation before anyone realizes there is a problem.

Janelle has learned that “I’ve got it handled” usually means Antoine has decided to carry something alone.

She wants a future with him, but not one built around waiting for the next crisis to drag him back into secrecy.

### Story Role

Janelle represents Antoine’s adult future.

Devin represents the younger person Antoine may be able to guide.

Janelle represents the kind of stable life Antoine may be able to build if he learns that honesty and trust are also forms of strength.

She should not exist simply to threaten to leave Antoine whenever he makes a mistake. Her role is to know him well enough to recognize when his old habits are returning before Antoine admits it himself.

# HEARTLINE

* Origin: Taiwan
* Type: International pop group
* Language: Mandarin Chinese / additional languages as appropriate [tentative]
* Playable: No
* Role: Emotional anchor / central point of convergence

## Background

HEARTLINE is an internationally successful Taiwanese pop group whose popularity has expanded beyond Taiwan through touring, online fandom, and international festival appearances.

The group is scheduled to perform at a major music festival in New Orleans.

Their appearance attracts significant attention because HEARTLINE is one of the festival’s major international acts.

The members of HEARTLINE themselves are not responsible for the criminal scheme surrounding their tour.

Instead, the scale of their success has created a large touring operation involving management, equipment crews, contractors, transportation companies, security staff, promoters, temporary workers, and local event partners.

That complexity has allowed someone inside the logistics or management structure to exploit the touring system for personal gain.

International production expenses have been deliberately inflated, with excess money routed through cooperating contractors and partially returned to the insider.

Because HEARTLINE moves large amounts of equipment and money between countries, suspicious costs can be disguised as normal touring expenses.

A whistleblower inside the touring operation eventually discovers the pattern and begins collecting evidence.

Before HEARTLINE leaves Taiwan for New Orleans, the whistleblower hides physical proof inside an obsolete equipment case from one of the group’s previous tours.

That case travels with the rest of the group’s equipment to Jazz Fest.

## Connection to Nia and Annika

For Nia and Annika, HEARTLINE has nothing to do with crime.

The group is part of their friendship.

The girls watch performances together, argue about their favorite songs and eras, recognize old costumes and logos, and dream about seeing HEARTLINE live.

Their plan to attend HEARTLINE’s Jazz Fest performance becomes one of their shared promises.

After Annika dies, HEARTLINE becomes emotionally inseparable from Nia’s memory of her.

Nia decides to attend the performance for both of them.

Because she and Annika were such devoted fans, Nia knows HEARTLINE’s visual history unusually well.

She can recognize old tour branding, logos, equipment designs, colors, and other details that adults around her would consider meaningless.

That knowledge eventually allows her to recognize that an older HEARTLINE equipment case does not belong among the group’s current touring gear.

## Connection to Antoine

HEARTLINE initially means very little to Antoine personally.

His connection develops through the people around him.

Nia loves the group because of Annika.

The New Orleans fixers connected to Antoine’s past are hired to retrieve evidence hidden somewhere inside HEARTLINE’s equipment.

Those fixers manipulate Devin into helping them by arranging legitimate temporary festival work and asking him to move a particular equipment case.

HEARTLINE therefore becomes the point where Antoine’s responsibilities toward Nia and Devin collide with the life he has been trying to leave behind.

## Connection to 陳柏安

For 柏安, HEARTLINE initially represents work.

His investigation begins with a small security irregularity surrounding the group’s international travel and festival preparation.

As he examines credentials, contractors, equipment records, and suspicious access, he discovers financial irregularities that suggest HEARTLINE’s international touring operation has been exploited for a larger criminal scheme.

柏安 follows the investigation to New Orleans because he understands which records and credentials may already have been compromised.

His involvement with HEARTLINE therefore creates the professional obligation that once again interrupts his relationship with his daughter.

## Story Role

HEARTLINE is not the protagonist of **As One**.

The group is the point where the protagonists’ unrelated lives intersect.

For Nia, HEARTLINE is memory.

For Antoine, HEARTLINE is where his past begins threatening the people he cares about.

For 柏安, HEARTLINE is responsibility.

Their Jazz Fest appearance creates the environment where all three meanings collide.

HEARTLINE also supports the game’s central idea that the same object or event can carry completely different meaning depending on who is experiencing it.

A HEARTLINE equipment case can simultaneously be:

* a piece of touring equipment to an ordinary worker;
* a target to the fixer crew;
* evidence to 柏安;
* a mistake Devin wishes he had never touched;
* and an instantly recognizable piece of HEARTLINE history to Nia.

That difference in perspective is central to **As One**.
